I am pleased to submit our original research manuscript entitled "Personalized ECG Stress Detection with Subject-Specific Baseline Normalization and Bare-Metal Edge Evaluation" for consideration for publication in Biomedical Signal Processing and Control.

This work addresses a fundamental bottleneck in wearable affective computing and the Internet of Medical Things (IoMT): inter-individual physiological baseline heterogeneity and the risk of unencrypted telemetry. Using single-lead ECG from the benchmark WESAD cohort (N=15), we demonstrate that leak-free subject-specific relative baseline calibration provides a statistically significant +11.53 percentage-point gain [95% CI: +5.07, +18.06] in classification accuracy under rigorous 15-fold Leave-One-Subject-Out (LOSO) cross-validation at N=10 calibration windows, achieving 90.17% accuracy [95% CI: 82.7%, 95.9%] and 90.49% F1-score [95% CI: 81.9%, 96.2%] on 295 non-anchor evaluation windows. Furthermore, we demonstrate bare-metal execution on an ARM Cortex-M4 microcontroller (STM32G474RE) performing real-time 5-stage Biquad filtering in 1.476 µs (251 mean cycles, 0.0517% CPU load) and evaluating 10,000 physical hardware-in-the-loop (HIL) telemetry packets over UART with zero CRC errors and zero packet loss.

This manuscript is original work, has not been published previously, and is not currently under consideration for publication elsewhere. All authors have read and approved the manuscript.
